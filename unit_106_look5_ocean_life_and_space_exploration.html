Life Beyond Earth: The Dream of Space Exploration

For centuries, humans have looked up at the night sky with curiosity, wondering whether life exists on other planets. Today, that curiosity has become a powerful force driving space exploration. Astronauts and scientists send unmanned rovers to gather data on distant worlds. On Mars — the fourth planet in our solar system — robotic rovers like Curiosity have discovered evidence that liquid water once flowed on the planet's surface. Scientists believe this means Mars may have once supported simple life forms. Meanwhile, the International Space Station orbits the Earth at approximately 400 kilometres above ground. Crewmates living aboard the ISS follow a strict schedule: they check equipment, conduct experiments, and exercise for two hours a day to prevent their muscles from weakening in low gravity. When astronauts float inside the station, they experience how truly different life without gravity feels. The rocket technology that makes all of this possible is constantly improving. Spacecraft take off with enormous force, carrying oxygen, food, and scientific tools. Many experts make the prediction that humans will land on Mars before 2050. Whether crewed or unmanned, each mission brings us closer to answering the mesmerising question: are we alone in the solar system?
