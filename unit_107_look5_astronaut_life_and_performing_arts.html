A Day in the Life of an Astronaut

Life on the International Space Station is very different from life on Earth. Every day, astronauts must follow a strict arrangement of activities to stay healthy. Because there is no gravity, it is impossible to run outdoors, so astronauts use a running machine for about two hours each day. They also have to pull themselves along rails to move from one part of the station to another. Outside the station, the atmosphere is a mixture of gases, and beyond our planet lie the other worlds of our solar system: stormy Jupiter, icy Uranus, ringed Saturn, and distant Neptune. Astronauts can observe these planets through a telescope or by studying data sent from probes. Carbon dioxide levels inside the station are carefully monitored because too much of this gas is harmful. Mission Control on Earth stays in constant contact with the crew. When a mission ends, the whole team at Mission Control celebrates the touchdown of the spacecraft. Back on Earth, many astronauts become performers of a different kind — they go on stage at schools and theatres to share their incredible stories with enthusiastic audiences, inspiring young people to dream of the stars.
