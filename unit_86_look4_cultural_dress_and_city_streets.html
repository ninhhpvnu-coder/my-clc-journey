A Journey through History, Architecture, and Desert Attire in Prague

Last autumn, our history teacher organized an unforgettable educational tour to Prague, the enchanting capital of the Czech Republic. As our group strolled along the cobblestone pavement, the morning sun began to come up behind ancient Gothic towers. We stopped before a historic manor where an ornate iron gate guarded the grand entrance. A courteous caretaker used an antique brass key to open the heavy door, revealing a magnificent entrance hall with polished stone floors. He explained that this building once used to belong to a rich merchant who traded rare textiles and gemstones across Europe and the Middle East. Inside the gallery, exhibition cases displayed traditional desert garments, including a white thobe, an embroidered abaya, and a protective woollen shesh. In the museum boutique downstairs, a friendly shop assistant helped a customer who asked, 'How much… ? How much is that handcrafted cone-shaped lamp in medium size?' Afterwards, we took a lift to the upstairs library, where scholars studied geological surveys of subterranean caverns adorned with delicate stalactites hanging from ceilings and towering stalagmites rising from the cave floor.
