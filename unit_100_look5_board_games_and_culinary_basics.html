Mastering the Board on the Steppes: A Tale of Resilience and Strategy

Living on the sweeping grasslands of Central Asia, twelve-year-old Altan and his sister Sarangerel lead a life of remarkable resilience. During the bitter winter months, temperatures outside their traditional round ger can plummet to fifty degrees below zero, while summer brings burning sunshine across the dry steppe. Their nomadic family owns a wooden cart pulled by a sturdy ox, which helps them transport essential supplies and simple wooden furniture whenever they migrate. Inside the cozy ger, their mother prepares healthy meals using wholesome wheat flour, creamy yak butter, and sweet mountain honey, occasionally seasoning hearty stews with black pepper and dried herbs. Despite the remote wilderness, Sarangerel developed an extraordinary intellectual passion: she loves to play chess. Every evening after she turns off the solar lantern, she visualizes each (chess) piece moving across the sixty-four squares of the board. She trains intensely hard, sometimes playing completely blind against her brother to sharpen her mental concentration and spatial memory. Last month, Sarangerel decided to enter a competition organized in the national capital. Supported by her entire village, this gifted female prodigy hopes to represent her school in an international championship next year. Commentators agree that she is probably one of the most promising young minds in the country, proving that strategic brilliance can flourish anywhere.
